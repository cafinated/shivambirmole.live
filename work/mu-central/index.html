        Ten messages. Six of them matter tomorrow.

        An illustration of the idea, with invented messages. The real thing is at mu-central.vercel.app, behind a campus email sign-in.

        What I noticed

        I am a student at Masters’ Union. The campus runs on group chats, and three kinds of message keep getting buried in them: a notice everyone needs, a thing someone lost, and a thing someone wants to sell.

        How I thought it through

        Each of the three wants a board that stays put, so each got one. I kept them in one product so there is a single place to check.

        The gate mattered more than any feature. A marketplace and a lost-and-found only work if you trust the other person is from your campus, so access is limited to verified campus email addresses.

        What I built

        Three boards behind one sign-in: notices, lost and found, and buy and sell. I built it alone, from the database to the deploy, with Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind and Supabase, hosted on Vercel.

        Where it stands

        The site is live and the code is public. I have no usage numbers to share.

        What I’d explore next

        Whether people will open a separate place when the group chat is already in their hand. That is the question the product has to answer, and I do not know yet.

        What is known, and what is not.

        What I did

        • Designed and built the whole thing alone.
        • Deployed it on Vercel.

        What the product supports

        • A notice board.
        • Lost and found.
        • A buy-and-sell marketplace.
        • Sign-in limited to verified campus email addresses.

        What has been measured

        • Nothing yet.

        What I do not know yet

        • How many students use it, and for which board.
        • Whether a separate place beats the group chat people already have open.

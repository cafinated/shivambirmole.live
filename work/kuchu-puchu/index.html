What I noticed

Most long-distance friendship runs through a chat window. The message that says “thinking of you” arrives in the same place, and in the same shape, as the one about a delivery slot.

How I thought it through

The product’s bet is that delivery matters. A character that walks onto your desktop, waves, and tucks itself back into a corner is a different event from a notification.

Two choices follow from that bet. Puchu, the AI companion, drafts a message but you press send, so the words stay yours. And receiving is always free, so nobody is locked out of a hello.

What I built

A desktop app for Mac and Windows, in four parts.

Greetings. Pick a friend and a gesture. Your character walks onto their desktop and acts it out.

Characters. Little people and creatures you can dress until they look like you.

Sparks. A five-second scene that fills your friend’s whole screen, with your note in the middle.

The Couch. Each person plays their own copy of a film, and the app keeps everyone in step. One pauses, everyone pauses.

Where it stands

Kuchu Puchu is public and downloadable. This page reports no usage numbers and makes no claim about what it does for the people who use it.

What I’d explore next

Whether a greeting that is delivered feels closer than one that is typed, and which of the four parts people come back for.
